This is a great problem focus. Anyone who travels knows how confusing, scattered, and challenging it can be to get up-to-date reliable information in a way that's accessible. Moreover I think a general solution to this problem could become a standard through airports worldwide. Try finding an airport that would share openly with you and is interested. This could be an interesting angle - be cautious of the time constraints.  

## Overall

The airport proposal starts from concrete questions people currently have to answer across several sources. The example conversation and the division between flight data, documents, and website information make the intended experience understandable.

## Could Be Stronger

I'd select a small initial set of public documents and one flight source, then state what the prototype can answer when either is unavailable. The feasibility milestone is the right place to settle those dependencies. I'd also expand the evaluation beyond routing accuracy: include questions that require two sources, documents that disagree, and flight responses that are old or missing. Matching an API response is useful, but the answer also needs to communicate when that response was retrieved and whether it is the right flight. Keep mock-feed demonstrations visibly distinct from live status.

## Distinctive Strength

Your plan to ask a clarifying question when routing is uncertain is a good fit for this audience. A passenger shouldn't need to understand which internal agent owns a question. The source-linked response can keep that internal complexity out of the conversation while still letting the user check the answer.
