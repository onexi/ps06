## Overall

World Wide Search has a clear motivation: useful work can be missed because a researcher doesn't know the right language or terminology. I liked that the proposed output is a visible, reproducible search process with sources for human review.

## Could Be Stronger

The next step is to name the pilot domain, three languages, I'd be tempted to only do one, and exact connectors, including a fallback if one isn't accessible. Those choices determine whether the project can test the search gaps you describe. I'd also tighten the success criterion. Finding one source someone might have missed is promising, but difficult to judge consistently. Use a small fixed set of research tasks, the same time allowance for each approach, and a reviewer who can assess relevance across the selected languages. Record useful new sources and setup time separately.

## Distinctive Strength

You recognize that translation alone doesn't solve discovery. Separating language, search-engine coverage, and differences in terminology gives the project a more interesting question to investigate. The decision to leave synthesis and automated conclusions out of the first version protects that focus.
