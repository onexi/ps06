## Overall

Read2Discover gives the user a simple starting point and a concrete result: reading material in, five explained recommendations out. Two required sources and one retry make the planned search loop much easier to build and evaluate within the course.

## Could Be Stronger

I'd define who judges relevance and what the 80% target means across your 10–15 scenarios. Give the keyword baseline the same sources and retrieval budget so the comparison tests the agent's planning rather than extra access. Also clarify what happens when one source fails or produces no useful matches. Requiring two formats shouldn't force an irrelevant item into the feed; a shorter, clearly explained result may serve the reader better. Include those cases in the evaluation rather than only testing topics both sources cover well.

## Distinctive Strength

Balancing relevance with diversity is a good choice for discovery. Your two-subtopic target makes it possible to notice when five individually relevant results all repeat the same idea. The short explanation for each match also gives readers a way to understand and challenge the agent's interpretation of their interests.
