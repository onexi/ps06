## Overall

Idea Diligence addresses a useful problem: helping founders identify what they need to learn before investing more effort in an idea. Your assumption map and example experiment make the proposed output concrete, and researching only the top five assumptions gives the first version a manageable boundary.

## Could Be Stronger

I'd make the implementation plan more specific by naming the initial technologies, model, and search sources, along with a limit on calls or cost. The architecture explains the responsibilities well, but leaves those resource choices open. The competitor slide also refers to sources in speaker notes that aren't visible in the submitted PDF; include those references in the PDF so the comparison can be checked.

I'd also define who judges an overlooked high-risk belief and what makes an experiment useful. Choosing an experiment doesn't yet show that it will answer the important question. Use a short rubric across all three conditions, with outputs reviewed in shuffled order, and distinguish participants' preferences from evidence that the proposed tests would resolve an uncertainty.

## Distinctive Strength

You explicitly recognize that internet research cannot prove customer demand. Keeping unsupported assumptions open and proposing a next experiment is a thoughtful boundary. It makes the tool's value about improving the founder's next decision, rather than making a polished report look like validation of the business.
