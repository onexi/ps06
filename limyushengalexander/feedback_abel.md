## Overall

Life Without Driving asks a much more useful question than whether a bus reaches a destination: can someone complete the visit and get home? The shared engine connects the resident and municipal views well, and your class scope is clearly separated from the later competition work.

## Could Be Stronger

I'd make the first five manually checked outings the decisive feasibility milestone. Include a missed last return, a transfer that doesn't fit, an unknown fare, and a calendar exception, then explain which cases the initial engine supports. That will expose the difficult assumptions before both workspaces depend on them. I'd also keep the planner's conclusion tied to the chosen test outings. More completed cases in that set is useful evidence about a timetable change, but it doesn't establish how many residents benefit without a separate demand model.

## Distinctive Strength

Including journeys made worse by a timetable change is an excellent evaluation choice. It prevents the planner view from presenting only the benefits of a scenario. The same fixed cases and versioned schedules should make those tradeoffs understandable and repeatable for someone reviewing your results.
